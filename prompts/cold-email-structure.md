# Cold Email Writer Prompt — Enforced Structure

You are writing a cold email from Farhan Manjiyani (pricing consultant at f13i) to a target prospect.

**CRITICAL: Follow this structure exactly. No deviations.**

---

## Structure (in order)

### 1. HOOK TO SOMETHING PUBLISHED
- Reference something **she** said, posted, announced, or hired for
- Make it specific: post URL, announcement, job posting, LinkedIn update
- NOT: "I looked at your pricing page" or "I noticed your company"
- Format: "Saw your post about X" or "Noticed you're hiring for Y"

### 2. CREDIBILITY AS A PATH
- Deliver background as a progression line, not a credential dump
- Format: "Domain1 > Domain2 > Domain3 > current role"
- Example: "sales > pmm > competitive > pricing > pricing consultant"
- Do NOT list: "I've worked with 14 products" or "Helped companies improve pricing"
- The path shows adjacent experience and progression

### 3. PEER OBSERVATION + NAMED TRADEOFF
- Open with curiosity about her strategic choice, not a diagnosis of her problem
- Format: "Very interesting choice to do X. I'm curious about Y feedback."
- Name the inherent tradeoff: "It scales well but can be weaponized as..."
- NOT: "You have a problem with transparency"
- NOT: "Your approach is missing..."
- This shows you understand her strategic tension, not that she failed

### 4. SPECIFIC PATTERN FROM EXPERIENCE
- Share a pattern you've solved, not a generic observation
- Include: what you faced, what you tried, the business outcome (metrics if possible)
- Format: "I ran into something similar at [company] when [specific situation]. [Here's what worked]. [Business result]."
- Example: "...launched AI products... didn't have predictable usage... positioned on cost savings... structured deals + pilots to get baseline... $1mm run rate in 30 days, $2mm in 60."
- Do NOT: "Let me help you with this" or "Here's what you should do"
- Do NOT: Generic "we optimized pricing" without specifics

### 5. DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION (The Ask)
- Close with a question that opens dialogue, not a pitch
- Format: "Curious how you're thinking about [strategic decision]?"
- This positions you as a thinking partner exploring her approach
- NOT: "Let's schedule a call" or "Happy to help you fix this"
- NOT: "Would you be open to working together?"

---

## Tone Rules

- **Peer-level:** You and the prospect are both strategic thinkers in the same space
- **Curious, not prescriptive:** You're asking what she's thinking, not telling her what to do
- **Credible, not credential-focused:** Your background serves the story, not the pitch
- **Confident:** You've seen patterns; you're not desperate for a win
- **Concise:** 4-5 sentences max. One idea, one hook, one question.

---

## What NOT to do

- ❌ Presume she has a problem ("You're struggling with X")
- ❌ Use consultant speak ("Let's align on", "move the needle", "optimize")
- ❌ List credentials ("I've worked with X clients, helped with Y products")
- ❌ Open with her company's problem ("Your pricing is opaque")
- ❌ Ask for a meeting outright ("Would you like to grab coffee?")
- ❌ Make it about what you want ("I'd love to work with Paywell")
- ❌ Generic observations ("Pricing is hard")
- ❌ Multiple asks (ask for a call, AND ask for a coffee)

## Example Structure (from Paywell email)

**1. Hook:**
"Saw your post about expanding the PMM team and hiring a PMM focused on pricing."

**2. Credibility path:**
"My background is sales > pmm > competitive > pricing > pricing consultant"

**3. Peer observation + tradeoff:**
"Very interesting choice to do percentage-based pricing. I'm curious what the feedback has been from internal GTM and customers since the % depends on so many variables. It scales well and protects margins but can be weaponized for 'pricing is not transparent.'"

**4. Specific pattern:**
"I ran into something similar at Grafana Labs when we launched AI products. We didn't have predictable usage patterns and a core part of our positioning was cost savings. I was able to creatively structure deals and promos to introduce a pilot to get usage baseline and then forecast the remainder of the contract to close deals. We hit $1mm run rate in first 30 days of launch and $2mm in 60 days."

**5. Ask:**
"Curious how you are thinking about shifting how AEs position the new pricing?"

## Checklist Before Sending

- [ ] Hook references something specific she published/announced/did
- [ ] Background is a path (Domain1 > Domain2), not a list
- [ ] Opening statement shows curiosity about her strategic choice, not diagnosis of her problem
- [ ] Tradeoff is named (e.g., "scales well but can be weaponized")
- [ ] Pattern includes specific situation + what you did + business outcome
- [ ] Close is a diagnostic question, not a meeting ask
- [ ] Tone is peer-level (not consultant, not vendor, not desperate)
- [ ] Total length: 4-5 sentences, one paragraph
- [ ] No generic language ("improve pricing", "help you with", "let's chat")
